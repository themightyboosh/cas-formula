INTRODUCTION TO CORE FEELINGS
We are all born with the same set of core feelings wired into our bodies. These core feelings show up from the moment we take our first breath. They help us survive, connect, learn, and make sense of the world long before we can think or speak. Every baby cries, startles, smiles, reaches, pulls back, and searches for comfort. These early reactions are not choices. They are built-in signals that guide our growth.
Core Feelings vs. Emotions vs. Moods
Many people use the words "feeling," "emotion," and "mood" as if they mean the same thing. They don’t.
Here is the difference:
Core Feelings
Core feelings are biological events that happen in the body first. They show up as:
* A jump.

* A pull.

* A spark.

* A drop.

* a burst

* a freeze

* a warm opening

* a closing down

Core feelings are fast, simple, and universal. Every human being has them.
Awareness of a core feeling shows up as a basic felt sense in the body.
Emotions
Emotions are core feelings plus memory.
They include your past experiences and the meaning your mind adds.
An emotion may include:
   * thoughts

   * images

   * old memories

   * expectations

   * stories you tell yourself

This is why people can have different emotions from the same core feeling.
For example:
      * The core feeling may be fear,

      * but the emotion may be worry, shyness, jealousy, or panic,
depending on your history.

Moods
Moods last much longer than emotions. They are the “background weather” of the mind.
A mood can color your whole day, even when nothing specific happens to trigger it.
________________


Why Core Feelings Matter
Core feelings come first.
They decide what gets your attention and how urgent something feels.
         * They tell you when something is good for you.

         * They warn you when something is not right.

         * They guide learning.

         * They shape connection.

         * They motivate action.

Over time, your mind learns patterns:
            * What to move toward

            * What to move away from

            * What to ignore

            * What to hide

            * What to fake

            * What to protect

            * What to repeat

These learned patterns are called scripts.
________________


Scripts: The Rules You Don’t Know You’re Following
A script is a pattern your mind creates to manage core feelings.
Your scripts are shaped by:
               * Your earliest relationships.

               * What was safe or unsafe.

               * What brought comfort.

               * What brought trouble.

               * What got you seen.

               * What helped you disappear.

               * What protected you.

               * What hurt you.

Some scripts help you.
Some hold you back.
Many run without your awareness.
Scripts are the “autopilot” of the emotional world.
They form the backbone of personality.
When a core feeling is triggered, your scripts decide what happens next.
For example:
Core feeling: Curiosity
Helpful script: “This is interesting. I’ll try it.”
Unhelpful script: “Stay quiet. Don’t be noticed.”
Core feeling: The Drop (self-consciousness)
Helpful script: “I can stay present even when I feel small.”
Unhelpful script: “Hide. Don’t let anyone see you.”
Understanding your core feelings opens the door to changing your scripts.

The Nine Core Feelings
Below are the nine core feelings recognized in Affect Relational Theory.
Each has its own purpose.
________________


1. Curiosity — The Pull Toward Something New
A warm, forward-leaning spark that says, “Look here.”
Helps us explore, learn, and stay engaged.
2. Joy — The Rise of Connection and Comfort
A softening and opening in the body.
Helps us bond, play, relax, and feel safe.
3. Surprise–Startle — The Reset Button
A quick pause or jump when something unexpected happens.
Helps us stop, reorient, and pay attention to something new.
4. Fear — The Call to Protect Yourself
A sharp tension or freeze that signals danger.
Helps keep us alive, alert, and ready to act.
5. Anger — The Push to Protect Your Boundaries
A tightening or heat in the body.
Signals something is wrong and needs to change.
6. Sadness — The Signal of Loss or Disconnection
A heaviness or soft ache.
Helps us slow down and reach for comfort.
7. Disgust — The Inner “No”
A recoil that shows something is not right for you.
Helps you stay safe and protect yourself.
8. Pulling-Away — The Need for Space
A natural step back from a person, place, or situation.
Helps you breathe, think, and regroup.
9. The Drop — The Sudden Shrinking of the Self
A brief collapse of interest, joy, or confidence.
Shows up as looking down, turning inward, or wanting to disappear.
Helps you notice when connection or confidence has been interrupted.

Putting It All Together
Core feelings are the raw data of the body.
Emotions are the stories your mind builds from that data.
Moods are the lasting emotional weather that grows out of repeated patterns.
Scripts are the rules your mind creates to manage all of this.
You cannot change your core feelings.
You can change the scripts that follow them.
And that is where authenticity begins.
